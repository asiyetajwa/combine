Combine: Evidence Log

Solar-assisted compute for small LLM labs. What we have checked, what we have not, and what comes next, in the open.

Honest status, 2 November 2026: Combine is a research and prototyping project. Public evidence today is an interactive concept, an assumption-based energy model, and the model results below. Measured hardware results and confirmed deployments are still to come. No labs are testing Combine today, and nothing on this page should be read as claiming otherwise.

Sprint log

2 November 2026: Evidence sprint begins

We started a six-week evidence sprint, organised around Stage 1 of our proposed EDA flow: set energy, latency, memory and accuracy targets from a measured system baseline, using system models and workload traces. From today, every change in the evidence is published on this page the same day.

Two commitments frame the sprint:

  • Simulation first, honestly labelled. Models are published with their assumptions, so any reviewer can rerun them and disagree with us precisely.
  • Ten small-lab interviews. We are interviewing ten independent builders and small LLM labs about compute access, cloud cost, power reliability and data privacy. Interviews are demand evidence and design input. Interviewees are not testers, pilots or users, and we will not describe them that way.

2 November 2026: Energy model v1

Our first reproducible system model: an hourly, year-long energy-budget simulation of one Combine node (PV array, battery storage, inverter, one accelerator workstation), across 54 scenarios: three solar irradiation levels (4.0, 5.0 and 6.0 kWh/m2/day, following the bands in our NASA POWER based solar atlas), three array sizes (2, 4 and 6 kWp), three storage sizes (5, 10 and 15 kWh usable), and two scheduling strategies for flexible fine-tuning work (a fixed evening start, versus placement in the sunniest hours).

Workload assumption, stated plainly: one 4-hour parameter-efficient fine-tuning session and 8 hours of local inference per day, about 2,442 kWh of load per year. Workstation power figures are assumptions based on a proposed 600 W class accelerator, not measurements. Replacing them with measurements is the next stage of this sprint.

What the model says:

  • Sizing: at 5.0 kWh/m2/day, a 2 kWp array with 5 kWh of usable storage already covers about 99.9 percent of the annual load on solar energy in the model. At 4 kWp, more than 40 percent of generated energy has no load to serve. For this workload, a bigger array is wasted capital; growth should go into more workload, not more panels.
  • Scheduling: in the tightest case modelled (4.0 kWh/m2/day, 2 kWp, 10 kWh storage), sun-aware placement cuts grid import from 186 to 85 kWh per year, at the cost of 15 deferred fine-tuning sessions the single daily slot cannot catch up. Scheduling trades grid energy for delay, and we report both sides of that trade.
  • Storage: moving from 5 to 15 kWh of battery changes grid import only slightly in most scenarios, because the load is daytime-heavy. Storage size should follow measured evening demand, not intuition.

Next for the model: a cross-check against pvlib and the NREL System Advisor Model, monthly seasonality from NASA POWER climatology, and then measured workload traces in place of assumed power figures.

What is not claimed

  • No measured hardware results yet. All power figures above are labelled assumptions.
  • No pilot, deployment, customer or lab partnership.
  • No EDA tools in current use, no completed RTL, no tape-out. The EDA flow is a proposed future flow; the tools listed there are prospective, and the application responses behind it are a draft, not a submission.
  • No relationship with any chip maker or AI company is claimed. Names mentioned in our materials are prospective customers or collaborators only.
